# The record of a session

`recordSessions().record(target)` (src/recorder.ts) writes one target's session, and `replay` (src/replay.ts) runs it again on a new object with every handler off. A record is the canonical print (canonical-print.md) of one value, so that file defines its bytes: UTF-8 JSON with no whitespace, and numbers, strings and every value in the forms of its table. The record's own objects (the top, a step, a call, a fired entry, an outcome) are written with their keys ascending, the order each is given in below; what they hold follows the print's **Keys** rule. Another implementation writes and reads the same bytes.

- **Source**: everything comes from the engine's tap (`FlowTap` in packages/core/src/flow.ts), per target. The tap pairs a step's end with its dispatch by the signal object and a late answer with its call by the call object; a record holds what that pairing gives, never an identity. An end with no step to pair with, of a dispatch made before the recording saw the target, adds nothing to the record; the works it lists as unsettled are still known, so their late answers are not recorded either.
- **Top**: `{"cannotReplay":<null or string>,"start":<states>,"steps":[<step>,...],"stop":<states>}`. Each `<states>` is what `printStates` writes for the target: an object that maps each state's name to its instance.
- **cannotReplay** is null, or `"a transition was pending where the record starts or ends"` when either holds: the last end paired with a step of the record that is not a refused abort's (its result's code is SF036 to SF039, packages/core/src/error-codes.json) is marked temp; or the first step is an abort, of a transition that started before the recording. A replay refuses such a record. A record whose target was told no step is never refused, as the tap tells nothing that shows a transition pending and the engine says it only through a promise (`sync`): written while one was pending, its start and stop hold the temp state, and a replay compares only them.
- **start** holds the states at the first dispatch of the target the recording was told, or at the call that wrote the record. **stop** holds the states when the record was written. The steps are the dispatches told from the call of `recordSessions` until its scope ends.
- **step**: `{"abort":<boolean>,"calls":[<call>,...],"fired":[<fired>,...],"payload":<object>,"signal":"<name>","states":<states>}`. There is one step per dispatch, in the order the flow reduced them, queued and muted dispatches included, and ones the reducers refused or ignored. `abort` is true when the dispatch was sent as an abort of a pending transition, accepted or refused, as the step's end says; false for an abort sent with nothing pending, which is dispatched as usual, and for a dispatch whose end never came (one that threw). The payload holds the signal's own enumerable string-keyed props; `states` are the states its dispatch found.
- **fired**: `{"node":"<path>","observer":"<name>"}`, one per observer, event (`event:<name>`) and watch (`watch:<callback name>`) whose decision asked for its callback in a step of the dispatch, in the order the engine lists them: those of the dispatch's own step, then, for a transition, those of its end.
- **call**: `{"kind":"exit"|"update"|"enter"|"transition","node":"<path>","outcome":<outcome>,"variant":"<state>.<variant>"}`. A call is appended to the step last dispatched to its target when the engine tells it. A transition's work is told when it settles, so it goes to its own step when it settles before the next dispatch to its target, and otherwise to the step of the last dispatch before it settled: an accepted abort it answered, or an abort refused while it ran. A work an end lists as not settled is appended, when that end is told, to the step of the end's dispatch, with the outcome `{"unsettled":true}`: the transition's own step after a timeout or another work's refusal, the abort's step after an accepted abort that dropped it. Its late answer, told later for the same call, is not recorded: the engine drops it. A call told for a target before its first step is left out. Several handlers of one call make one call, with the outcome of the first that gave something. Commit handlers are not recorded: they change no state.
- **path** is the node's path as the log names it: the state's name, then `.<prop>` for a nested state and `.<prop>[<key>]` for an Items entry, the key in its ECMAScript `String()` form (`media.producers[cam]`).
- **outcome** is null for OK and for Ignored; `{"data":<value>,"named":"<result>"}` for a named result; `{"data":<value>,"rejected":"<message>"}` for a refusal, `data` being what the handler gave with it (`HandlerResult.rejected(message, data)`), `{"$undefined":true}` when it gave none; `{"error":{"message":"<message>","name":"<name>"}}` for an Error; `{"unsettled":true}` for a work that had not settled when its transition ended or was dropped. A message is the text as the engine or the handler wrote it: an engine error's ends with ` [SFnnn] <fix>`, from packages/core/src/error-codes.json.
- **Time**: a record holds none. While a transition is pending, a replay moves its clock to the next timer due, one timer at a time, until the transition ends, so its timeout, or an accepted abort's wait for an unsettled work, ends it as in the session, with no real wait.
- **Numbers**: the whole record has one numbering, in writing order: `start`, then `steps`, then `stop`. In a step only `calls` (the data of named results and of refusals), `payload` and `states` hold values that take numbers, in that order. A value has one number wherever it stands.
- **Replay** builds the object with the record's start states and a new TestClock as its timers, compares the steps its build dispatches with the record's first ones, and sends the rest in order under one lock. Before a step whose `abort` is true it runs only what is due now; before any other step, and after the last, it waits until no transition is pending, moving the clock as **Time** says. It answers the calls of each variant and kind in the record's order: the n-th call the replay makes of a variant and kind gets the n-th outcome recorded for them, OK once none is left. A transition's work gives its answer only once the replay has sent the step its recorded call is in, the steps of the build once the build returned; a work recorded unsettled is never answered. It turns each value back as follows. Each number n becomes one stand-in, a new object of a class of its own whose dispose does nothing, so it prints as `{"$ref":n}` again. It holds nothing else, and the caller's build may give the stand-ins of the start states members, which no print writes: the ones the object's own code reads from the value outside a reducer. `{"$box":n}` becomes a Box of n's stand-in and `{"$disposableBox":n}` a DisposableBox of it. `$variant` becomes the variant named. An object of data keeps its key order, and a key written `$$k` is read back as `$k`. `$map` becomes a FrozenMap and `$set` a FrozenSet. `$state` is the named variant called on its props. `$items` is an empty map of the definition `of` names, filled with its pairs in order. `$number`, `$bigint` and `$undefined` become their values. A record that names a variant or a definition the replay was not given is refused before anything runs. A step whose dispatch throws on replay rejects the replay with that throw. A transition the build itself started ends before the first step is sent, by its works' answers or, moving the clock, by its timeout. The replay's own record is read after the last step settled, while its lock is still held, so a send of the object's own that waited for that lock is not in it. Then the lock is released and the replay waits until the object is at rest, as the engine's `settled` says it (nothing left to deliver, the locks asked for served, no transition pending), moving the clock from timer to timer meanwhile: the sends that waited for the lock run to their end on a live flow, a transition one of them declares included. Only then is the object disposed. A lock the object asks for only after a wait of its own is not waited for.
- **Returns**: `{ differences, replayed }`: the list of **Differences**, and `replayed`, the text of the replay's own record as read under the lock, in this format; a caller reads from it the states the object reached this time.
- **Differences**: one text per part that differs, `<where>:\n  recorded <JSON>\n  replayed <JSON>`, with `<where>` being `start`, then `step <i>` from 0, then `stop`. Each side is the part as `JSON.parse` read it and `JSON.stringify` wrote it again, so keys that are array indices come first, ascending, and the others follow in the record's order; a step one side lacks is written `undefined`. A refused record throws `replay: cannot replay this record: <cannotReplay>`; a name the definitions do not hold throws `<name> is named, which the definitions given do not hold`.
- **Limits**:
  - An Error comes back with its name and message, not its class.
  - A named result its variant no longer declares is not refused before anything runs: the answer throws where the handler ran, the engine takes that as the handler's Error, and the replay lists what differs.
  - An abort of a transition the build itself started, sent while it was pending, is not replayed as sent: the replay holds no lock until that transition ends, so the transition ends first, at its timeout, and the replay lists what differs.
  - A keyed set comes back as a map, so a reducer that adds to it with `with(item)` gives an Error on replay.
  - A hole (`{"$hole":true}`) is not turned back, so it replays as a difference.
  - A stand-in the build gave no members throws where the object's own code reads a member of a member of it, or calls one: in a watch's callback or an event's payload the engine sends the throw to console.error, and in a promise of the object's own it is an unhandled rejection. A stand-in that a step's payload brings first, under a number the start states do not hold, is out of the build's reach.
  - A record written before data kept its key order holds its data's keys sorted and names both kinds of box `$box`; nothing in a record says which it is, and a replay reads it as it stands.
  - With no time in the record, a timeout or a wait changed since the recording replays with no difference while the same thing ends the transition. No time passes between steps: a timer the object set itself fires only while a pending transition's wait passes it, and then every one due before that transition ends fires.
  - A flow the build applies on timers other than the clock given waits its timeouts and an abort's wait in real time on replay, as long as in the session.
  - Several works of one variant in one transition are answered in the order they settled in the session, not the order they were called.
  - When a target is disposed while a transition is pending, its work's late answer is recorded as an answer in the last step.
  - One signal object dispatched to one target again while the transition of its first dispatch is pending pairs the later ends with the second dispatch, so the record and its replay differ. Each dispatch takes its own signal.
